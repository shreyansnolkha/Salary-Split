The problem

The salary arrives, the rent and EMIs go out, and what is left gets spent one order at a time. Nobody decided how much of it was safe to spend. It just ran down until it ran out.

Tracking apps help after the fact. They are good at telling you where the money went, which is a little late when you are on the 18th and working out whether you can afford dinner.

The missing piece is a number you set at the start of the month: how much to lock away, and how much you are free to spend.

How it works

Three steps, one screen, about 60 seconds.

  1. 1

    Enter your take-home

    The amount that actually lands in your account. Switch between rupees and percentages whenever you like.

  2. 2

    Fill in five numbers

    Rent, EMIs, food, transport and everything else. Not sure of the exact figure? Put in a percentage instead.

    Rent and EMIs are your fixed costs. Food, transport and everything else come out of your free to spend.

  3. 3

    Get your plan

    One screen shows what to save, your fixed costs, your free to spend number and what that comes to per day.

    It also shows what you are saving today (your take-home minus those five numbers) against your target, so you can see whether you are on track or how much you are short.

The save figure comes from a 50/30/20-style benchmark, adjusted for metro rent.

What makes it different

The method is pay yourself first, in a fixed order. Savings, then fixed costs, then whatever is left. It is a plan, not a tracker.

  • It shows where money should go, not where it went. Most apps look backwards. This one gives you the plan in under a minute, before you have spent anything.
  • No bank access, no logging, no dashboards. Five numbers and a take-home figure. That is the whole input.
  • Rupees or percentages. Do not know your exact numbers? Use percentages. That excuse is gone.

What it will not do

  • bank login. It never connects to your bank and never asks for access.
  • investment advice. It tells you how much to lock away, not what to put it in.
